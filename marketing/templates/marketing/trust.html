Evidence and privacy

Keep the source.
Understand the finding.

Know who can access a case, which material a finding came from, and where processing happens. These are part of the current workspace.

Access belongs to each case.

Every case has one owner who manages invitations and viewer or editor access. Viewing evidence, downloading files, searching, and running background analysis require current case access.

Self-hosting uses the same account and case permissions.

Originals stay separate from interpretation.

Pandect retains original files and captured text separately from editable source details, derived findings, and review decisions. Checksums help track integrity; they do not establish whether the content is true.

Archiving retains evidence for case members. Deletion is a separate, confirmed action: owners can delete a whole case, and authorized superusers can review and delete individual evidence and its dependencies.

Evidence and retention guide ↗

Findings lead back to evidence.

Review the source passage, image region, recording interval, or observation records behind supported results. Processing versions and history remain available.

Recorded observations, derived patterns, and model suggestions stay distinguishable. A possible trip or matching identifier is a lead to examine; it does not establish a route, identity, or association.

Processing choices

Know where your material goes.

Local checks

Supported text extraction, file metadata, and printed-English image recognition run on the worker host. Image OCR never sends images to an external service.

Private model services

Other supported AI defaults to an explicitly configured local or private-LAN runtime. Missing or failed local processing does not trigger external fallback.

External AI by approval

External processing needs an operator-configured Cloudflare AI Gateway route, deliberate selection, and submission approval. The operator manages credentials, logging, and retention settings.

Maps have their own privacy choices.

Operators can configure private tiles and geographic services. External address or context lookups require approval unless the endpoint is explicitly designated local.

When external map tiles are configured, viewing a map shares your browser’s IP address, site origin, and viewed map areas with the tile provider.
